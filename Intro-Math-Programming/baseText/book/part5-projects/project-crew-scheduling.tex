\section*{The problem}
A hospital system (or airline, or warehouse) must cover a week of demand
with legal shift patterns: rest rules, maximum consecutive days, skill
requirements. The number of feasible patterns is astronomically large —
enumerating them all is hopeless, which is precisely the setting where
column generation and Lagrangian relaxation shine.

\section*{Learning goals}
Set-covering/partitioning formulations with exponentially many columns;
the restricted master problem and reduced costs; pricing as a constrained
shortest path / dynamic program; Lagrangian relaxation and the connection
between Lagrangian duals and LP relaxations of the master; branch-and-price
at a conceptual level; heuristic column pools.

\section*{Stages}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Data.} Demand curves per hour/day; a rule set for legal
    shifts. Start small enough to enumerate, then scale until enumeration
    breaks.
  \item \textbf{Compact model vs.\ pattern model.} Solve both on the small
    instance; verify they agree.
  \item \textbf{Column generation.} Implement the loop: restricted master
    (LP), dual prices, pricing DP, add columns, repeat. Track the bound.
  \item \textbf{Lagrangian view.} Dualize the covering constraints; compare
    the Lagrangian bound and the CG bound.
  \item \textbf{Integer solutions.} Rounding, diving, or a final MIP over
    the generated pool; report gaps.
\end{enumerate}
