\chapter*{Preface}

This book is a living document.  Feedback, questions, comments, and edits are welcome at \href{mailto:open.optimization@gmail.com}{open.optimization@gmail.com}.

This book is being written and compiled using a number of open source materials.   We will strive to properly cite all resources used and give references on where to find these resources.  Although the material used in this book comes from a variety of licenses, everything used here will be CC-BY-SA 4.0 compatible, and hence, the entire book will fall under a CC-BY-SA 4.0 license.  The Sources and Attribution chapter records, source by source, exactly what was used and under which license.

\begin{center}
\fontsize{14pt}{16pt}\selectfont\textcolor{titletextcolour}{\textbf{MAJOR ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS}}
\end{center}

I would like to acknowledge that substantial parts of this book were adapted from the following open-source materials.
All content used is compatible with the CC~BY-SA~4.0 license of this book.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{``A First Course in Linear Algebra''} by Lyryx Learning (based on original text by Ken Kuttler), licensed under \textbf{CC~BY~4.0}.  Their formatting and templates are used throughout, along with selected sections that make up the linear-algebra appendix.  We are extremely grateful to Lyryx for sharing their files with us.

\item \textbf{``Foundations of Applied Mathematics''} with many contributors, licensed under \textbf{CC~BY~3.0~US}.  See \url{https://github.com/Foundations-of-Applied-Mathematics}.  The one-dimensional optimization material and the Gradient Descent Methods chapter in the nonlinear programming part are adapted from these labs, along with several figures.

\item \textbf{``Linear Inequalities and Linear Programming''} by Kevin Cheung, licensed under \textbf{CC~BY-SA~4.0}.  See \url{https://github.com/dataopt/lineqlpbook}.  Sections of the integer linear programming material are adapted from these notes.

\item \textbf{Wikipedia}, licensed under \textbf{CC~BY-SA}.  A small number of passages and figures are adapted from Wikipedia articles; each use is attributed where it occurs and in the Sources and Attribution chapter.
\end{itemize}

I would also like to acknowledge Laurent Poirrier and Diego Moran for contributing various notes on linear and integer programming.

I would also like to thank Jamie Fravel for helping to edit this book and for contributing chapters, examples, and code, and the students who contributed pioneer spotlights and examples throughout.
